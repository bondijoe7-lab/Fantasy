Reliability-weighted draft model

DRAFT BOARD // 2026

Rankings and tiers built from opportunity share, regression-adjusted efficiency, offensive context, age curve, and durability. Every projection is scored in true half-PPR math — 0.5 points per reception, explicitly broken out below. Toggle league size to see replacement level and VBD shift under the same tiers.

Half-PPR scoring formula (exactly what's applied to every player)

Passing: 0.04 pts / yard + 4 pts / TD − 2 pts / INT Rushing: 0.10 pts / yard + 6 pts / TD Receiving: 0.10 pts / yard + 6 pts / TD + 0.5 pts / reception
Worked example — Ja'Marr Chase, projected ~102 receptions: that alone is ~51.0 points from the reception bonus before a single yard or touchdown is counted. Swap to standard (0-PPR) scoring and Chase loses those 51 points while a low-target, high-efficiency runner like Derrick Henry loses nothing — this is why the model, and the real market, value pass-catching volume more heavily as PPR credit goes up.

Backtest findings (2012–2024 nflverse data) that still shape this model

Tested whether each year-N factor actually predicts year-N+1 half-PPR points, out-of-sample, position by position:
opportunity β efficiency β age β R² RB (n=677) 0.53 0.07 −0.16 .31 WR (n=1091) 0.64 0.09 −0.15 .41 TE (n=542) 0.62 0.04 −0.09 .37 QB (n=301) 0.24 0.29 −0.10 .16
For RB/WR/TE, opportunity predicts next season 7–15× better than efficiency does — the model still leans on volume heavily for those positions. QB is the opposite — efficiency (accuracy, TD rate, INT avoidance) predicts about as well as volume, which is why QB keeps a separate weight set with opportunity and efficiency near parity. Regression-to-the-mean is real too: players in the top TD-rate quartile one year fall roughly 40–50% of their excess back toward the mean the next year. Age is no longer a separate weighted term — see below for why — but the fitted age curve is still computed and shown in the Age column.

The blended model — six inputs, not five

Every player's Reliability grade and point projection now blend the backtest-fit model with two independent external signals: CBS Sports' expert consensus and real sportsbook markets. Base weights:
RB / WR / TE: opportunity 30% · CBS consensus 20% · Vegas 15% · efficiency 15% · durability 10% · context 10% QB: opportunity 22% · efficiency 23% · CBS consensus 20% · Vegas 15% · durability 10% · context 10%
QB keeps opportunity and efficiency close to parity (22/23) rather than the skill-position split, preserving the backtest finding above. Age was dropped as its own term — CBS's experts and the betting market both already price in a player's age and decline risk, so weighting age separately on top of two signals that already reflect it would double-count the same information.
Coverage rule: CBS consensus has a floor score so every player gets one; Vegas usually doesn't exist for a given player (~20 of 156 skill players have a real posted season prop). Wherever a factor has no real data for a specific player, that slice is dropped and the remaining weights are rescaled back up to 100% for that player — nothing is filled in with an invented number.

Opportunity 30%*

Volume — targets, carries, red-zone touches, snap share. Backtest-confirmed as the dominant predictor for RB/WR/TE.

Efficiency (regressed) 15%*

TD rate and yards-per-touch, pulled toward the position mean. Backtest-fit weight — much smaller than volume for skill positions.

CBS Sports consensus 20%

Averaged rank across CBS's PPR and standard top-200 (this league is half-PPR, so it sits between the two), converted to a percentile.

Vegas markets 15%

Real season-long prop lines and league-leader futures, converted to implied points where a real number exists. Dropped and rescaled where it doesn't.

Offensive context 10%

Each team's real 2026 season win total (Rams/Ravens 11.5 down to Cardinals/Dolphins 4.5), rescaled 0–100 — complete for every player.

Durability 10%

Games-missed history and current injury flags. Judgment-based weight — no clean public injury-designation dataset exists.

Tiers, VBD, and FLEX logic unchanged: tiers cut at the largest point gaps within each position; VBD nets out the last realistic starter for your league size — now computed against the fully blended point projection.

How CBS consensus enters the point projection

The Mkt column shows live ADP when available (green dot), or CBS's averaged rank otherwise. But CBS also directly shapes pj: a player's CBS positional rank is looked up against this model's own point-sorted list at that position, converting "CBS's opinion" into real points on this model's own scale — rather than just displaying it as a side-by-side comparison. Model and CBS genuinely disagreeing pulls the blended number toward the middle; agreeing reinforces it.

How Vegas enters the point projection

Where a real stat-line prop exists (pass/rush/rec yards, TDs), it's converted to points via the same half-PPR formula and scaled up to an implied full-season total using a documented share constant (e.g. rushing yards ≈ 42% of a typical RB's season total). One exception: a raw receptions prop only gets converted to points for WR/TE, never RB — a bell-cow runner's reception count says nothing about his (dominant) rushing value, so that conversion was actively wrong when tested and is excluded rather than left in. "League leader" futures odds (a probability, not a stat line) feed the Reliability grade only, never the point projection directly.

Kickers & defense

K/DST scoring is heavily game-flow and matchup dependent — the least predictable positions in fantasy. They're included with simplified projections and get almost no draft capital priority; most rosters should stream them or take one in the final two rounds.

Live data vs. model updates

The "Mkt" column pulls live ADP from Fantasy Football Calculator's public API on every page load — that number updates itself automatically, no action needed. The underlying player quality model (opportunity/efficiency/tiers/projections) doesn't auto-update, because that takes real judgment about depth-chart news, injuries, and role changes — not just a live number. Come back and ask for a refresh anytime new information changes the picture.
